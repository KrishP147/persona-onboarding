# 15. the graduation card, and a yes you can take back

*sept 27, 2026*

setup used to end with a sentence. "done with setup, you're in." that's fine, but nothing on screen showed what it had just learned, or let you take any of it back. so the end of setup is now a card in the thread, drawn by whichever phone you're on.

## what i know about you

four rows: what you call it, your name, what you need help with, and gmail. each has edit and forget (the agent's own name only has edit). edit doesn't open a form. it drops "actually, call me " into the composer and puts the cursor at the end, because the conversation is the interface and a correction should look like one. forget asks once, inline ("forget your name?" yes / cancel), then tells the server, which wipes the slot and, for gmail, the tokens. rows you never gave just say "not shared". under it, up to three things it would do first from your inbox, then the footer: "yours to delete. gone means gone."

- **show the model of you.** don norman calls it the system image: people build their idea of a system from what it shows them, not from what it does inside. a card that lists what it knows is the honest system image (*the design of everyday things*, 2013, ch. 1).
- **forgiveness over warnings.** norman again: make actions reversible and errors cheap, instead of guarding every step with a dialog. forget asks once, small, in place; edit costs nothing (ch. 5).

## send, with five seconds to change your mind

a draft email used to arrive as a long text. when gmail is connected it's now a card: to, subject, a few lines of the body, then send / not yet, and "nothing happens without your yes." send starts a five second bar with undo. only when the bar runs out does it text "yes, send it" through the normal chat path, so the server's own send checks still run. undo, or leaving the page, sends nothing. after setup the card sits right under the what-i-know card, so the loop closes in one place.

- **undo beats "are you sure".** raskin argues confirmation dialogs train people to click yes without reading, and that undo is the humane default (*the humane interface*, 2000, ch. 6). a short delay with a visible undo is the same idea for things that can't be unsent once they leave.

## smaller things

- first visit shows a short note for reviewers: what this is, use chrome or edge for the call, and three things to try. it never blocks the opening texts, which arrive underneath. esc or a tap outside closes it and it stays closed; `?intro=1` brings it back.
- reasoning is off by default and remembered. (this used to be a folded lane of cards beside the phone; 17 replaced it with a map you open from a pill.)
- when the server sends them, the checks that ran on a reply show as small shield chips beside its node in the map, and the node's detail has the decision process panel with turns, cost and p50 for the session.
